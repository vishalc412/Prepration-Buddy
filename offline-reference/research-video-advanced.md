# Verified video companions for advanced interview days

Verified on **2026-10-09**. The companion dataset contains **20 real watch URLs** across days 15–30, with creator attribution, a focused viewing task, an offline practice task and a limitation for every selection. Nineteen selections directly target a curriculum skill or case; the twentieth is a deliberately labeled historical BitNet research overview. Videos are optional enrichment. The static lessons and exercises must remain sufficient when the user has no internet, model endpoint, downloaded model or provider account.

## Selection and verification

Identity and topic were checked against first-party creator channels, university course pages or publisher pages. Public YouTube oEmbed metadata additionally resolved exact titles and creators where normal page rendering hid the metadata. Publisher embeds were used to resolve watch IDs; none were guessed. YouTube oEmbed returned HTTP 401 for the three Stanford selections during the final metadata pass; their exact identities/topics were independently confirmed in the official Stanford channel search results and the university course pages. Other selections resolved through oEmbed or publisher embeds. This does not assert live playback for every viewer. Verification means **identity and topic**, not a claim that every second was watched, that a resource is current in every API detail, or that the complete video has been independently audited. No durations or unverified timestamps are supplied.

The watch/practice tasks are curriculum exercises written for this project. They are not claims that the video contains those exact exercises. All practice tasks can use local Python, Java, fixed strings, synthetic measurements or hand-worked diagrams. Provider demonstrations remain optional.

## Explicit gaps and scope limits

**Focused BitNet implementation video:** A suitable focused first-party YouTube tutorial for BitNet b1.58 was not established in this search. The verified Microsoft talk above is a 2023 research overview that explicitly names BitNet. It must remain labeled supplemental; it predates the [b1.58 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17764) and is not evidence that b1.58 training or deployment is taught. The [official Microsoft BitNet repository](https://github.com/microsoft/BitNet) is a written reference, not a substitute video. Do not relabel generic quantization instruction as BitNet instruction. A [creator-authored community post](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/bitnet-b1.58-2B-4T/discussions/20) additionally links AI WITH Rithesh architecture/testing videos; their metadata identities were checked, but they are community material, not Microsoft-endorsed instruction. They were not substituted for the research overview because the title claim about no floating-point operations needs careful interpretation and technical depth was not independently established in this pass.

**Complete financial-controls video:** FinBot supplies a relevant attack demonstration, but it does not establish comprehensive instruction in ledger integrity, reconciliation, payment approvals, accounting standards or regulatory compliance. The offline day-30 content must teach those mechanisms directly through deterministic simulated examples. Do not present a CTF as operational banking guidance.

**Full OWL standards coverage:** The selected Neo4j episode supports basic ontology inference. It should accompany the local lesson's RDF/OWL distinctions, open-world assumptions and inference examples; it does not certify OWL 2 completeness.

**Optional network use:** Viewing YouTube requires connectivity and platform availability. Every answer, hint and exercise belongs in the static project. Avoid automatic external requests or embedded players that become prerequisites. A user should be able to choose a companion video later without losing the local six-hour study path.

## Suggested teaching integration

Use a resource as a companion to a lesson, not as the answer key: read the offline explanation, attempt the worked example, optionally watch with its specific task, then complete the local practice and teach-back. Split the six-hour day across explanation, exercises, lab work, interview answers and review. Watching alone is not a mastery signal. Saved practice results, failed reasoning checks and independently explained examples are more useful evidence.

The JSON uses stable local IDs and the topical keys requested by the project. Every record has an explicit limitation and `verification: "identity-and-topic"`. It does not contain measured watch durations, model-quality claims, remembered prices or inferred timestamp ranges.
